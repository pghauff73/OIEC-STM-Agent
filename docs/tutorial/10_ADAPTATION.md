# Adaptation

## What you will learn

You will adapt one declared dimension while preserving lineage, boundaries, and evidence requirements.

## Everyday analogy

A proven recipe may be scaled from four servings to eight while preserving ingredient ratios. Replacing the cooking method and ingredients at the same time is a new recipe, not one controlled adaptation.

## New vocabulary

An **adaptation dimension** is the one declared property allowed to change. **Lineage** records what the candidate came from. A **confirmed gap** names missing qualified coverage.

## Diagram

The diagram shows a qualified source record, one permitted adaptation edge, unchanged invariants, and a candidate awaiting new evidence.

## Command or interaction

Choose one adaptation dimension in the sandbox. Attempts to change a second dimension produce a deterministic refusal.

## Expected output

The candidate records its parent, exact changed dimension, unchanged constraints, required experiment, and non-promoted status.

## What just happened?

Adaptation reused known structure without hiding novelty. The result remained a candidate until comparison evidence qualified it.

## Try changing this

Select a different single dimension and compare the evidence requirements. Then deliberately choose two dimensions in the invariant exercise.

## Common mistake

Lineage does not make a candidate correct. It makes the relationship to prior qualified work inspectable.
